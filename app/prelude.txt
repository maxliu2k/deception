Prelude
Rolph was deep into enemy territory. And that might've been a big deal if the enemy hadn't massacred themselves a few years ago. Although, if anything, the Darkness was probably more dangerous without anyone to guard it. Rolph tried to block out the memories of what it had done to him last month, but some thoughts still slipped through. You're too weak. You can't fix it. You'll never be strong enough… Then, a more recent one resurfaced. A warmer one. That, he held on to.
He was already more powerful than the Overseer. If he wasn't powerful enough, who was? A name floated in the back of his head: Creation. That begged a different question. If Creation was strong enough to fix the problem, why hadn't he done it in the three thousand years of Tellura's existence?
The Darkness was everywhere now. It had been growing ever since the birth of Tellura, beginning on the outskirts of kingdoms, slowly cutting them off from each other. By now, even the densest cities had been at least somewhat infiltrated, and it was clearly getting worse. Rolph had decided that he would be the one to fix this. After all, no one else had stepped up, not even the Overseer, the lineage ‘divinely chosen by Creation’ for that exact task. And he was the most powerful mortal on the face of Tellura.
Right now, though, the Darkness had him surrounded. It honestly wasn't a big deal. He motioned for it to go away, and it did. It was the benefit of knowing dark magic; you could protect yourself and those close to you from the void tendrils, at least to some degree. You failed at that last month, said the voice in his head. Rolph tried his best to ignore it. I won't fail again, he promised himself. He held the warmer thought closer.
Rolph arrived at his destination, shutting the door on some tendrils that attempted to follow him inside. The room was oddly void-free compared to its surroundings, and seemed to carry a warm presence, despite being in the middle of a desolate wasteland.
There was a giant pentagram inscribed on the floor, with candles scattered across the room, a singular oval mirror hanging on the far wall. Rolph stared at himself in the mirror. He looked as ordinary as always. Nothing special here, at least on the outside. He felt a sudden urge to kneel on the pentagram. The Devil was calling.
‘The Devil’ was probably not a good name for the face. Lucifer, in all reality, seemed like a friendly guy who got on the wrong side of Creation. Allegedly, he was confined to Hell after using his position as High Seraph in crimes against Creation, but really, that could mean everything. Plenty of other powerful people had been condemned in the same way, including Hyperion, the fallen Deity of Light, who was sentenced to eternal, isolated torture in Hell for refusing to obey Creation’s command to kill someone. But the mention of either of their stories was still highly taboo on Tellura. After all, history is written by the victors.
Lucifer was known on Tellura only as a deal-maker. His deal was actually quite simple. Upon entering Hell through his altar, you would duel him, and if he deemed you worthy, he would grant you power. Otherwise, you would be sent back to the surface, where you could live your life as normal, the only caveat being that your soul would be guaranteed to go to Hell after death. 
It sounded scary on paper, but Rolph knew it wasn’t at all in practice. The requirements to get into Heaven were so ridiculously lofty that quite literally everyone was sent to Hell, so the second clause of the deal was effectively worthless. The deal was actually just a shot at being blessed by the Devil, although that phrasing made it sound much more sinister than it actually was.
Rolph knelt on the pentagram, and within moments, the room disappeared around him. A few seconds later, he reappeared in seemingly the same room, kneeling on the same pentagram, with the same walls, although everything now had a warm tint, and the dim candles had now all been replaced by air conditioning units. Somehow, despite the array, it was still quite hot in the room, although Rolph appreciated Lucifer’s attempt at hospitality. Where Rolph had seen his reflection just seconds before, he now saw the Devil himself.
Lucifer himself looked completely ridiculous. And by that, Rolph meant that Lucifer looked exactly like he was pictured in the sketches from books. Rolph really didn’t understand why people were afraid of this guy. He had the most generic haircut that Rolph had ever seen, wore a silly little suit and had the goofiest grin possible plastered on his face, twirling his trident nonchalantly with one hand.
“Ah, it’s Rolph Dundgren! I’ve been waiting for someone like you to show up for something like two thousand years! Pleasure to be meeting you!” Lucifer remarked, right before being blasted into the wall by a ball of magic. The trident clattered onto the floor. “Ow! What the heck—”
“Shut up and fight,” said Rolph, before crashing into Lucifer, sending them both tumbling through the wall, landing outside. It was swelteringly hot out in the open, and Rolph understood why so many AC units were needed inside the room.
“Hey! What!? You’re not supposed to be able to leave that room!”
“I was never one to be confined by rules.”
“That’s not—possible?” Lucifer glared at the wall as if it had offended him. Before he could turn and react, however, Rolph barreled into him again, sending him flying into a crowd of demons. The demons scattered slowly, watching in confusion and awe.
They lunged at each other. Light met light head on. The explosions were bigger than any attacks Rolph had ever seen, and consequently, he felt more adrenaline than ever. For once, an attack broke through Rolph’s shield, sending him flying backwards. He almost immediately got back on his feet.
“I suppose Eve wasn’t lying, then! You truly are as powerful as she claimed you were. I’d say I’m impressed, but you don’t seem much for talking today…”
Rolph broke through Lucifer’s guard and crashed into him, sending them flying through Hell. They crashed into a building, where there seemed to be a party going on. Sinners spilled drinks everywhere while attempting to flee the scene. 
Lucifer grabbed Rolph by the collar and threw him out of the building. “Okay, let’s try to at least stay out of civilian territory, alright?” Rolph ignored the command.
Beams of magic arced through the backdrop, a spectacular light show, and as they flew through Hell, sinners and demons peered out their windows to witness the two glowing embers blazing across the sky.
Rolph felt greater than ever, and for the first time in a month, he was genuinely smiling. The duel felt almost dancelike. He waltzed around fireballs and sunbeams. Seemingly in an attempt to protect the towns below from the carnage they were creating, Lucifer fought from below, leaving Rolph the high ground, and it was becoming increasingly easy to take advantage of. In fact, Rolph was beginning to have fun. He shined a completely non-offensive light spell downwards, blinding Lucifer so badly that he fell out of the air.
Lucifer fell through a tarp, landing in a cake. Demons all around screamed at the sudden appearance of wings in their dessert. He brushed the frosting off his suit as he attempted to deflect more magical theatrics away from the ceremony.
Lucifer seemed to realize his tactical mistake, and began fighting more evenly, attempting to corner Rolph back into the room. Rolph fell back, feinting a retreat into the altar room… before intentionally crashing headfirst into the room below.
An orange-haired girl yelped in surprise from her bed in the corner, dropping her phone. “DAD!? What in Creation’s name!? I’ve told you like, a THOUSAND times to stop crashing into my wall as a ‘surprise’! It’s not funny and—” she paused, realizing that Rolph was not her father. 
Lucifer followed shortly behind through the same hole in the wall, looking genuinely angry for the first time. His eyes were flickering from normal to red. “That’s enough. You’ve proven yourself worthy. There is no need to keep destroying things.” Rolph ignored this command as well.
Without another word, magic pinned Rolph down onto the floor, neutralizing his attempts at offense, as more and more chains of light wrapped around his arms. “Stop squirming. It’s no use; the chains are unbreakable,” Lucifer commented, watching Rolph struggle. However, ‘unbreakable’ sounded awfully black-and-white. And nothing is truly black-and-white.
Lucifer tried to make a sarcastic comment about the whole ordeal to the girl, and got as far as the first word before the chains exploded in a bright blaze of colors, charring the room in hot energy. The last thing Rolph saw before the flash was Lucifer spreading his wings to shield his daughter.
After the light died down, Rolph took in the spectacle of the steaming, singed room, before a mildly burnt Lucifer struck him with the trident, pushing him back into the open skies. 
They fought their way to the end of Hell, leaving a trail like a shooting star blazing across Hell’s sky. Neither of them were holding back anymore, but the outcome was already crystal clear. More chains of light began trying to wrap around Rolph’s arms, but they were repeatedly severed by Rolph’s fluid blades of dark magic, a technique he was never supposed to learn. Lucifer, seeing this, looked like he just saw a dog walking on two legs. 
He began trying to use the trident more to guard his weak points, but that was perfect for the fluidity of dark magic to maneuver around. Black, choking smoke filled the air as Lucifer’s light and fire were slowly drowned out. 
Within minutes, the King of Hell was fully bound up by a cocoon of light and dark magic, plummeting into a huge rock below. He was covered in giant wounds, and his suit was now more golden than white. Rolph let go of the spells. Lucifer groaned as he stood up slowly, gripping the ridges of the boulder for support.
“This is…” he said, panting heavily, “ridiculous… How is it even possible for a mortal to be this strong!?! I suppose, actually… it’s more amazing than ridiculous. I hope you fight with this level of resolve up there too. Perhaps you’ll be the one who finally accomplishes what we’re all waiting for…”
Rolph smiled, also covered in quite a few gaping slashes. For the first time in a month, he made a joke. “I disagree. I don’t think I’ll ever discover the formula for the Elixir of Life.” 
Lucifer laugh-wheezed. “You know what I’m talking about, Rolph Dundgren.”
“I really don’t think I do.”
“Alright, let’s skip this charade.”
“No, no, don’t go on yet, I’m really confused. What are ‘we’ all waiting for?” Rolph tilted his head.
“Huh. Aren’t you aware of the whole… Darkness problem?”
“‘Darkness’? What’s that? Sounds frightening.” Rolph stifled a giggle.
“Okay, okay, serious talk now—”
“Hey! You can’t just ignore my questions like that. It’s really rude, and—”
Lucifer slapped him with the back of his hand.
“Alright, alright. I’ll stop.”
“As I was saying… uhhh, well, I don’t really have anything to give you. You are already more powerful than I am, so nothing I do would be of much use. I can only wish you the best of luck on the surface.”
“You could let me borrow that trident,” Rolph stated bluntly, motioning at the shiny relic. “Seems pretty useful to me.”
Lucifer clutched the trident closer. “I… don’t plan on giving this away anytime soon…”
“Why not?”
“It… it’s kind of the last and only thing I brought down from Heaven after, well, you-know-what happened.”
Rolph paused, formulating his words. “Well then that begs the question: do you still truly want to make a difference?” A wide smile stretched across his face.
The words struck Lucifer like lightning splitting a tree. Lucifer’s entire body froze, except for his mouth, which opened and closed repeatedly, although no sound was made. “I mean… if you want to put it that way, I suppose… I suppose you’re right…”
Lucifer handed over the three-pronged spear reluctantly, not really letting go until it was firmly in Rolph’s grasp. “Keep it safe, will you? I’d rather it not be returned in pieces.”
“Oh, no guarantees of course,” Rolph said playfully, twirling the weapon around with a certain mastery that even Lucifer didn’t possess.
Lucifer offered a hand, and Rolph took it gladly. Together, they took a nice stroll back to Lucifer’s tower.
“So… I suppose this is kind of a weird question to ask now, but… what exactly does Tellura think of me right now?”
“Most people know you as the fallen seraph turned deal maker, although, in my opinion, the books describe you mostly accurate to what I’m seeing right now,” Rolph chuckled, scrutinizing Lucifer’s questionable fashion decisions.
Lucifer brightened slightly. “Well, I suppose that’s better than I hoped for.”
“You’re still known as ‘the angel who had an illicit relationship with a mortal’, though.”
Lucifer squinted his eyes. “That’s… that’s just great. Way to spoil my fun.”
“So, in tangent, how are things with Eve going?”
“Huh. I guess neither of Eve’s siblings recorded that part? She hasn’t talked to me in… two thousand something years and counting. We basically only communicate through our daughter now. I still wish I could apologize to her, but… she’s clearly trying to avoid me,” Lucifer sighed. “I try not to think about it too much.”
“The girl with the orange hair. That was your daughter, Ruby?”
“How do you know her name?”
“I know a lot of things that I’m not supposed to.”
“Well, you’re right. Although at this point even ‘daughter’ might be a stretch. Basically every time we talk now, it becomes an argument in less than five minutes…” Lucifer hesitated, stopping himself from continuing. “That’s enough about the woes of my family for today. Eve said–well, Ruby said Eve said–that you married the greatest woman on our plane of existence.” He smiled hazily, daydreaming about how perfect his life, how perfect his family would be if he were in Rolph’s shoes.
But that thought hit an infinitely more dissonant chord in Rolph’s headspace. “She… isn’t with us anymore…” he stuttered.
Lucifer covered his mouth with his hands. “Oh, oh dear Creation. I am so sorry. What—what happened?”
“What else could possibly have happened to an elite noble from the Shining Citadel?” Rolph summoned a void tendril beside him, twirling it around his fingers. “We don’t just die off randomly. You’re right about the Darkness, Lucifer. I don’t see why William is still blind to quite possibly the most dangerous force in the universe. It would seem that the Darkness would have to pluck every last one of his beard hairs out before he pays it any attention.”
By now, they had arrived to the bottom floor of Lucifer’s tower. They could still see two gaping holes on the top floors from where the duel had occurred as they entered the elevator.
Lucifer sighed. “I see. Unfortunately, that’s just how it is sometimes. Although, aren’t you trying to become the next Overseer? Hopefully, with a bit of luck, you’ll be in the position to fix it.”
“Hah! ‘Trying’ is a bit too soft of a verb, I’d say I’m already in the ‘executing’ stage,” Rolph remarked cryptically. 
Lucifer had a few guesses to what that could mean, but he didn’t want to throw an accusation. His own sins were not so different. They stood in dead silence (except for the merry tune playing from the elevator) for a few seconds.
“By the way,” Rolph added, “sorry if I seemed a bit crazy back there. I’ve done… basically nothing except sit around since her death. Haven’t trained. Haven’t talked to anyone. Haven’t eaten very much either.”
“No, no—you’re fine. Don’t be too stressed about things. You’ve already got enough on your plate,” Lucifer consoled him, although Lucifer was subconsciously trying to internalize this mantra as well.
The elevator door rang, and they were back in the altar room. “Anyways, I stand by what I said earlier. It was truly a pleasure meeting you; you certainly didn’t fall short of any of my expectations. I wish you the best of luck on the surface. I guess this is goodbye, then? See you in the afterlife!” Lucifer waved.
“Don’t be so pessimistic about our next meeting date. Is there a rule against coming down here again—” Rolph began, before abruptly being transported back to the Rift. “—just to visit a friend?” he finished, before realizing that Lucifer didn’t hear that part.
Rolph opened the door slowly, inhaling the freshly cold air. Despite being quite injured, he felt a newfound sense of relaxation, as if the skies had finally been lifted off his shoulders. He looked at the trident in his grasp. In the pale-blue background of the Rift, it gave off almost a neon green glow, as if it clearly did not belong here. Perhaps it was right.
*
        At the edge of Hell, the Deity of Light was chained onto the floor. The chains were just slack enough to give him the illusion of being able to move, but not more than an inch. What seemed like a few hours ago (Hyperion actually had absolutely no clue how long ago; time feels different when you’ve been waiting around, doing nothing for three thousand years), Lucifer had crash landed nearby, seemingly dueling someone really powerful. Hyperion didn’t catch much of their conversation, but he did catch the name, and Lucifer’s praise. If I do ever get out of here, perhaps it will be because of this Rolph Dundgren, said a little voice in his head. Aww, look at you, Hyperion. So cute, still thinking that you’ll be free someday, replied another.
*
Several years later, William’s death came without warning. The weight of the title fell onto ready hands, but not onto a ready world. The world required motion, and so Rolph moved, though the machinery resisted every step. It was quite probably the most tiring job on Tellura.
And yet, from time to time, he still descended to Hell—not for power, not for bargains, but to speak to an old friend as if the world above were not steadily coming apart.
        Once, over dinner, Lucifer asked how his trident was treating Rolph.
        “The trident? I haven’t really used it for combat. It didn’t take me long to figure out that I’m absolutely terrible with melee weapons. It’s been hanging on a wall in my room.”
        Lucifer squinted at him. “Uh huh…” he paused, calculating his words. “In that case, could I have it back?”
        “No.”
        “Why not?”
        “Because every time I look at it, I remember what I’m fighting for. What we’re all waiting for.”
